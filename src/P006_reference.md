## Approved expression

How many people can live comfortably on Earth? The answer to that question today is quite different from what it would have been five hundred years ago. We have developed technologies that have increased the amount of food we can produce, the energy we can harness and the materials we can use to build our civilizations.

There is, however, a limit to how much of the Earth we can appropriate for ourselves without affecting the other biological systems on which we depend. In 2023, a group of scientists published research in which they concluded that we had exceeded six of the nine planetary boundaries they had established.

I think there is an important distinction between how many people the Earth can sustain and how many people can live comfortably on Earth. I would like us to continue improving the quality of human life, and I believe there are limits to how much we can do that on a single planet.

Coincidentally, at a time when we are becoming increasingly aware of these limits, we are developing the technologies that will allow us to expand into the solar system.

In 2021, we produced oxygen on Mars from the carbon dioxide in its atmosphere. The amount we produced was relatively insignificant. But we did not have to take that oxygen from Earth. If we can produce oxygen, extract water, harness energy and manufacture materials on another planet, we can begin to construct some of the infrastructure necessary for humans to live there.

I am not suggesting that we have the technology today to build a civilization on Mars. There is quite a difference between sending a few humans to Mars and establishing a population of humans who can sustain themselves there.

We should also consider whether the technologies we would have to develop to live on Mars could help us live more comfortably on Earth. Imagine having to design a community of ten thousand people that could not depend on another community for its water. How would we collect it, use it and recover it? How much water would we actually need? We have many communities on Earth that could benefit from the work we would have to do to answer those questions.

I don't see becoming multiplanetary as a means of escaping the problems we have created on Earth. We will have to become considerably better at managing resources before we can establish civilizations elsewhere in the solar system.

For the first time in our history, we can seriously contemplate expanding the physical boundaries within which human civilization exists. I think we should participate in that expansion.
